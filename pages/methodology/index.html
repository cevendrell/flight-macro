Methodology

From route deltas
to macro signals.

Every signal on the map is derived by comparing route-level flight volume across time and then attaching an economic reading calibrated against independent macro data. The pipeline runs daily and every step is auditable.

01

Ingest

Route-level frequency and seat capacity from OpenSky, BTS T-100, and Eurocontrol. Same-month prior-year snapshot for comparison.

02

Normalize

Adjust for known one-offs: aircraft grounding, airport closures, sanctions windows, holiday shifts. A raw +12% that's actually a base-effect deserves a footnote, not a headline.

03

Rank

Score each corridor by |delta%| × log(volume). Big swings on big corridors win; small routes with big percentages get demoted.

04

Attribute

Top corridors get sent to an LLM alongside macro context — FX moves, PMI, visa or policy changes, sector news — which proposes a plausible driver. This is a hypothesis, not a proof.

05

Score

Assign a confidence level based on how well the attribution fits corroborating data.

Confidence levels

HIGH MEDIUM LOW

High — reading is corroborated by at least two independent macro indicators moving in the expected direction.
Medium — one corroborating indicator, or a well-established prior for this corridor.
Low — plausible reading, but the data doesn't strongly rule out alternatives.

Themes

  • Tourism — leisure demand; sensitive to FX, disposable income, seasonal weather narratives.
  • Business — corporate travel; leading indicator for sector-level activity and cross-border investment.
  • Migration — one-way tickets, long stays, student and work visa corridors.
  • Supply chain — cargo capacity and business travel to industrial hubs; picks up nearshoring and rerouting.

Data sources

Prototype uses illustrative mock data. Production draws from published aviation statistics (OpenSky Network, US DOT BTS T-100, Eurocontrol) — all free. Sources for each signal are surfaced in-card as coverage expands.

Caveats

Flight data is a proxy, not a measurement. A 6% swing is a story to investigate, not a conclusion. When the map disagrees with official statistics, the map is usually earlier — and sometimes just wrong.
